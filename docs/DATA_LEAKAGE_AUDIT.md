## 7. Verdict

**Molecule level (same canonical SMILES in train and test)**
- Predictive 5-metal cohorts: 0.00% of test rows under StratifiedKFold (max
  0.00%), 0.00% under DOI-grouped 10-fold. No molecule-level leak.
- CFM v3: 0 SMILES carry more than one split label; canonical train/test
  overlap 0. No molecule-level leak.
- NCI-60: canonical train/test overlap 0 under the legacy protocol and 0 under
  the coverage default. MoleculeNet: max canonical train/test overlap 0.
  DrugOOD train vs ood_test: 0. No molecule-level leak in these splits.

**Scaffold level (same Bemis-Murcko scaffold in train and test)**
- Predictive random CV: 74.80%-85.73% of test rows share a BM scaffold with
  train (DOI-grouped 10-fold: 45.70%-64.24%). This is expected for random CV,
  but the number is not a scaffold-generalisation estimate.
- CFM v3: in-file `scaffold_id` overlap 0. The split key is stereo-aware and
  covers the whole complex. Removing stereo alone leaves 30 shared scaffolds
  (4.18% of test rows); removing counterions as well leaves 222 (73.71% of
  test rows). Most of the gap comes from counterion and solvent fragments, not
  stereochemistry. Train vs val is worse: 79.63% of val rows share a desalted
  ligand scaffold with train, and val clouds are what the CFM quality metrics
  are measured on.
- MoleculeNet: key aligned 2026-10-19. The split key is the stereo-blind
  `includeChirality=False` key (the `astartes`/chemprop reference), so the
  `split-key BM` and `stereo-blind BM` columns are one measurement and both
  are 0 by construction. The pre-fix key kept chirality; its test-set turnover
  and old overlap figures are in
  `metrics/by_metric/moleculenet_scaffold_key_alignment.json`.
- NCI-60: split-key BM overlap 0 (legacy) and 0 (coverage); 0 and 0 scaffolds
  span two splits respectively. Neither protocol leaks a scaffold.
- Pt 4-cell: the loader's `split_strategy` is named `scaffold_70_10_10`, but
  `scaffold_id` is a stable hash of the SMILES string, which makes it a
  molecule-level split. 172 shared desalted scaffolds (41.90% of test rows)
  and 40.82% of val rows; canonical overlap 0. Opt-in fix 2026-10-19:
  `bemis_murcko_70_10_10` gives a real scaffold split at 0.0% test-row overlap
  (`metrics/by_metric/platinai_4cell_scaffold_split_alignment.json`); it is
  not the default because it changes the training cohort.

**Cross-dataset**
- NCI-60 is a subset of MetalCytoToxDB: 5,207/5,207 canonical molecules are in
  the 5-metal pool and 5,183 are in CFM. It is a re-slice of the same corpus,
  not an independent control.
- Pt pool vs 5-metal training: exact canonical overlap 0; shared organic
  fragments 111. Pt is a different metal, so this is ligand-level overlap, not
  molecule identity. 296/3,555 unique Pt SMILES fail RDKit parsing and are
  compared as raw strings only.
- The Pt pool has no split column; it is a pool, not a train/val/test
  partition. Duplicate (code, cell) rows 3,668, exact duplicate measurements
  987.

**Protocol change, not leakage**
- The NCI-60 default split moved on 2026-10-21 from `scaffold_70_10_10` to
  `scaffold_coverage_70_10_20`, because the legacy protocol budgets fractions
  in scaffold counts and left 5 of 9 cells with fewer than 30 test rows. Both
  protocols are measured above. The shipped numbers (0.7294 m21, 0.6683 m3)
  were scored on the legacy protocol and reproduce only through the configs
  that pin it. No model has been retrained on the coverage split, and the two
  test sets differ by about 4.5 times, so the numbers are not comparable.

**Action**
- No re-split is required to remove molecule-level leakage; none was found.
- Two split labels did not describe their implementations and were aligned on
  2026-10-19. `train_moleculenet._bemis_murcko_scaffold` uses the astartes
  stereo-blind key, and the Pt 4-cell loader offers a real
  `bemis_murcko_70_10_10` strategy beside the legacy hash. The CFM file still
  claims a strict Bemis-Murcko split while its key is stereo-aware and covers
  the whole complex; that file belongs to the CFM session and remains open.
- Before reporting a scaffold or OOD number, group by a stereo-blind, desalted
  ligand scaffold (`_bemis_murcko_key` with `includeChirality=False`). For
  MoleculeNet comparability the reference key is astartes' stereo-blind,
  non-desalted key.
- The Pt pool is not partitioned, so it needs no scaffold split; it needs
  de-duplication before use as an external evaluation set. The synthetic
  4-cell cohort is partitioned but keyed by a SMILES hash. The real 4-cell
  cohort uses `scaffold_coverage` and shares no scaffold across splits.

## 8. MSRS strong-model splits (MET-10)

> Generated by `molmetal/scripts/msrs_data_card.py`; measured artifact
> `metrics/by_metric/msrs_data_card.json`. This section covers the corpus the
> strong regression model is trained on (21,404 potency rows). Sections 1–7
> cover the classification, generative, Pt and control datasets.

Each protocol controls one group key. The table reports the fraction of test
rows whose group key also appears in the training folds, weighted by rows over
folds. Per-fold ranges are in parentheses.

| Protocol | group key | ligand seen in train | DOI seen in train | scaffold seen in train | cell line seen in train |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `ligand` | `ligand_canonical` | **0.00%** | **89.43%** (84.82–91.33) | 71.17% | 98.40% |
| `doi` | `doi` | **15.43%** (13.43–19.20) | **0.00%** | 49.57% | 96.04% |
| `scaffold` | Bemis–Murcko | **0.00%** | **65.58%** (61.55–69.88) | **0.00%** | 97.76% |
| `year` | DOI year | 14.80% (5.21–26.95) | **0.00%** | 53.15% | 97.41% |

What the table shows:

- The `ligand` protocol does not measure generalisation. 89.4 % of its test rows
  come from a paper that is also in train. This accounts for the MET-2 drop from
  ligand 0.5556 to DOI 0.3321 (−0.2235, non-overlapping CI) and for the FIX-M4
  result that within-paper ordering creates decision-layer gains.
- The `doi` protocol is paper-disjoint but not ligand-disjoint: 15.4 % of test
  rows use a ligand that also appears in another paper's training rows. The
  measurement is independent, but the DOI number is not a cold-start ligand
  number.
- The `scaffold` protocol holds out the scaffold but leaves 65.6 % of test rows
  in a paper present in train. Its 0.4503 lies between ligand (0.5556) and DOI
  (0.3321), as a partially leaky partition should.
- Cell lines recur at 96–98 % in every protocol, so the model can learn a
  cell-line baseline from training rows. That does not corrupt within-cohort
  metal ranking, because the cell line is constant inside a cohort, but it
  inflates pooled R². Report pooled R² as a ranking model, not as a
  new-cell-line model.
- The `year` protocol drops 69.6 % of rows (14,905 with an unparseable DOI year,
  fold −1). It is the smallest and least stable protocol; its Δ-sign 0.4286
  (MET-3) rests on 49 pairs.

**Duplicates.** 304 keys on `(ligand, metal, cell, DOI, time)` are repeated
(663 rows, 3.1 %), 176 carry conflicting values (393 rows), and 142 rows are
exact duplicates. No duplicate key crosses a fold under any of the four
protocols, because the key is nested inside every group key. No de-duplication
is applied; the rule is in `docs/DATASET_CARD.md` §7.

**Censoring.** 5,015 potency rows (18.7 %) are right-censored and excluded from
training; 9 are left-censored. The model is fit on a complete-case subset and is
biased toward compounds potent enough to reach an exact IC50. This is a
selection limitation, not leakage, but it bounds every potency claim.

**Action.** No re-split is required for molecule-level leakage: the ligand and
scaffold splits are clean at their own key, and the DOI split is clean at the
paper key. The `ligand` number is the one that must not be quoted as
generalisation.
